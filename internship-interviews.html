Interview Preparation

At spring level most interviews are recorded video interviews. Here is how they work, how to structure an answer, and the questions to prepare.

1. The formats you'll face

FormatWhat happens
Recorded video interview
HireVue and similar. The most common at spring level.
You record answers alone, with no interviewer. Usually 3–5 questions, around 30 seconds to prepare and 2–3 minutes to answer each. A person watches the recordings afterwards. Used by JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and others.
Phone or video interview20–30 minutes with a recruiter or an analyst. Mostly motivation and behavioural questions, with a chance to ask your own.
Assessment centre
Less common for spring weeks.
Half a day: a group exercise, a short case study or presentation, and one or two interviews.

You usually get several days to complete a recorded interview. Use them: prepare your stories first, then record. Do not start it the evening it arrives.

2. The STAR framework

Every "tell me about a time when…" answer follows the same four steps. It keeps you to two minutes and stops you rambling.

SSituation

Where and when, in one or two sentences.

~15 seconds
TTask

What had to be done, and what your responsibility was.

~15 seconds
AAction

What you did, step by step. The longest part.

~60 seconds
RResult

How it ended, with a number, and what you learned.

~30 seconds

A worked example

Question: Tell me about a time you worked under pressure.

Situation. "In February our school investment club had to present at an inter-school event. Two days before, the student presenting our stock pick dropped out with flu."

Task. "As treasurer I was the only other member who knew the research, so I had to take over the presentation while still preparing for two mock exams that week."

Action. "I split the work in two. I blocked two hours each evening for the presentation and kept the mornings for revision. I cut our slides from fourteen to six so I could learn the argument rather than a script, asked a teacher to hear me once, and rewrote the opening after her feedback because it started with numbers instead of the story."

Result. "We came second of twelve teams, and the judges said our argument was the clearest. I learned that under time pressure, cutting the material down is faster than trying to learn all of it."

The same story, badly told

"We had a presentation and someone got ill so it was quite stressful, but we worked hard as a team and in the end it went really well and everyone was happy."

No detail, no actions, no result, and no "I".

Build a story bank

You do not need a story for every question. Prepare five or six strong ones in STAR form, and reuse them: most behavioural questions are asking for the same material from a different angle. Good sources: a club role, a work placement, a personal project, a sports team, a competition, a time something went wrong.

4. Recording a good video interview

  • Set up properly: camera at eye level (put your laptop on some books), a light in front of you rather than a window behind you, plain background, quiet room.
  • Dress as you would for the office: a suit or a blazer. It changes how you speak, too.
  • Look at the camera, not at your own face on the screen. Practise this: it feels odd at first.
  • Lead with your best material. The first 15–30 seconds decide how the rest is heard. Start with a one-line summary of your answer, then the detail.
  • Do not read a script. Notes as bullet points beside the camera are fine; reading is obvious and kills your delivery.
  • Practise by recording yourself on your phone and watching it back. Check your pace, your filler words and whether you finished inside the time.
  • Use the preparation time: 30 seconds is enough to pick your story and your first sentence.

5. Common mistakes

MistakeDo this instead
"I am passionate about finance."Name the moment you got interested and what you did about it.
An answer that would fit any bank.One fact that only fits this firm, from your company profile.
Saying "we" throughout a behavioural answer.Say what you did. The interviewer is scoring you.
Rambling past three minutes.Use STAR and stop at the result.
Pretending to know a technical answer.Say what you do know, then "I don't know, but I would find out by…".
No questions at the end.Have three ready; ask two.
